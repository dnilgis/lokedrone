What We Do

Ag Drone
Services

From fungicide runs on 500-acre corn fields to frost seeding pastures in early March — we operate year-round across Wisconsin and Minnesota. Both of our lead pilots hold Certified Crop Advisor credentials, which means every application comes with professional agronomy behind it, not just someone flying a drone.

01

Crop Spraying

Precise aerial application of fungicides, herbicides, and pesticides. Consistent coverage across any field, any terrain — without a tire track left behind.

  • Fungicide, herbicide & insecticide application
  • Get into wet or compacted fields
  • Zero soil compaction — no yield drag
  • 30–55+ acres treated per hour
  • CCA-advised rates and timing
02

Cover Crop Seeding

Aerial seeding directly into standing corn or soybeans. Establish winter cover without waiting for harvest — protecting your soil and extending your growing window.

  • Seed into standing crops — no harvest wait
  • Minimal soil disturbance
  • Uniform seed distribution across the field
  • Full-field coverage completed in hours
  • Reduced erosion and input costs
03

Frost Seeding

Take advantage of Wisconsin's freeze-thaw cycles in early spring. Establish pastures and forage crops without any ground equipment entering your fields.

  • Early March application window
  • No field entry or ground equipment
  • Soil freeze-thaw works seed in naturally
  • Cost-effective stand establishment
  • Alfalfa, clover, and mixed species

Yes. Both of our lead pilots are Certified Crop Advisors (CCA) — Sigurd Lindquist (CCA #813089) and Kevin Austin (CCA #736466). This isn't common in the drone spraying industry. Frost seeding is an early spring technique where seed is broadcast onto frozen or thawing ground. The natural freeze-thaw cycle works the seed into the soil without tillage or equipment. It's typically done in late February through March in Wisconsin, depending on conditions. We use aerial application — which means zero equipment in the field and no compaction during the sensitive early spring period.
After a successful hunt, recovering a deer in thick timber or rough terrain can be extremely difficult or impossible on foot.
